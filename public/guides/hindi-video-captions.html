How to Add Hindi Captions to a Video for Free

Hindi videos reach a much bigger audience when the words are on screen — most people scroll with the sound off. This guide shows how to generate accurate Devanagari captions with word-by-word timing, fix any mistake, and export a ready-to-post video in about five minutes.

Updated 13 November 2026 · 6 minute read

The tool used here is AutoCaption Studio, a free AI video caption generator that supports Hindi, Hinglish, English and 25+ other languages. Nothing needs to be installed: upload the file in your browser, review the captions, and download the result.

Why Hindi captions are worth the five minutes

  • Silent viewing: a large share of Reels, Shorts and feed videos play without sound, so captions are the only way the message lands.
  • Reach: Hindi and Hinglish captions make the video searchable in the language your audience actually speaks.
  • Retention: word-by-word highlighting keeps the eye on the screen, which helps people watch longer.
  • Accessibility: viewers who are deaf or hard of hearing get the full content instead of a guess.

What you need before you start

  • A video or audio file: MP4, MOV, WEBM, M4V, MKV, MP3, WAV or M4A up to 1 GB.
  • Clear speech. A phone recording is fine — clean audio matters more than camera quality.
  • A browser. There is no software to install and no account is required to export.

Step 1 — Upload the video

Open AutoCaption Studio and drop your file on the upload area, or click to browse. The file is uploaded only for transcription; temporary files are removed after processing.

Step 2 — Let the AI transcribe the Hindi audio

The server extracts a clean 16 kHz audio track with FFmpeg and sends it to Gemini for transcription. Hindi mode returns verbatim Devanagari text, for example “नमस्ते दोस्तों, आज हम बात करेंगे…”, with a timestamp for every word rather than one timestamp per sentence.

Word-level timing is what makes the karaoke and single-word caption styles possible, and it is also why editing a caption later does not knock the rest of the video out of sync.

Step 3 — Fix names and tricky words

AI transcription is strong, but brand names, English words inside Hindi sentences, and heavy background music can still cause mistakes. Click any caption in the editor and correct the text. The timing is redistributed across the words you changed, so you do not have to retype timecodes.

Three quick accuracy checks

  • Listen once at 1× speed and watch the highlighted word — it should match the audio.
  • Check numbers, dates and names; those are the most common errors.
  • If the audio is noisy, re-upload a version with the music lowered and transcribe that.

Step 4 — Style the captions

Three display modes cover almost every use case:

Pick a font that renders Devanagari cleanly (Hind and Poppins both work well), keep the text inside the platform-safe area, and check the preview at phone size before exporting.

Step 5 — Export the video or an SRT file

Two export options, two different jobs:

  • MP4 with burned-in captions — the captions are part of the video, so they show up on every platform and in every player. Best for Reels, Shorts and TikTok.
  • SRT file — a standard subtitle file you can upload to YouTube, import into Premiere Pro or DaVinci Resolve, or keep for later edits. Best when you still want to change the video.

You can download both: export the SRT first, then burn the captions into the MP4. Nothing is watermarked.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Too many words per line. Two or three words per caption line reads best on a phone; long lines get skipped.
  • Captions under the platform UI. Keep text above the bottom 15–20% of a vertical video so buttons do not cover it.
  • Ignoring the first two seconds. Start captions on the very first spoken word; a silent hook loses viewers.
  • Never checking the export. Watch the final MP4 once on your phone before posting.

Frequently asked questions

Is the Hindi caption generator really free?

Yes. Captions, styling and exports are free, with no watermark and no credit card.

Does it write Hindi in Devanagari or Roman letters?

Hindi mode writes Devanagari. If you want Roman letters (for example “aap kaise ho”), choose Hinglish — see the Hinglish captions guide.

How accurate are the captions?

Accuracy is highest with clear audio and little background noise. You can correct any word in the editor before exporting, so the final result is under your control.

Can I use the SRT file on YouTube?

Yes. Download the .srt file and upload it in YouTube Studio under Subtitles, or import it into any editor that supports SRT.
