How to Make Hinglish Captions for Reels & Shorts

Most Indian creators do not speak pure Hindi or pure English — they speak Hinglish. The captions should look the same way: “aaj ek chhota sa trick batata hoon” instead of Devanagari or awkward English spelling. This guide shows how to generate those captions for free and export them in a Reels-ready format.

Updated 13 November 2026 · 5 minute read

What counts as Hinglish?

Hinglish is Hindi speech written in Roman letters, mixed with English words the way people actually talk:

SpokenDevanagari captionsHinglish captions
आप कैसे हो?आप कैसे हो?aap kaise ho?
यार ये trick काम करता हैयार ये ट्रिक काम करता हैyaar ye trick kaam karta hai
आज हम बात करेंगेआज हम बात करेंगेaaj hum baat karenge

If your audience reads Hindi comfortably, Devanagari is the better choice — see the Hindi captions guide. For Reels, memes, comedy and creator content, Hinglish usually feels more natural.

Why most caption tools get Hinglish wrong

  • They force a single language: Hindi output comes out in Devanagari even when the creator wants Roman letters.
  • They transliterate word by word, so “yaar” becomes “yar” and “bhai” becomes “bhy”.
  • They put one timestamp per sentence, so the caption sits on screen too long and loses the karaoke effect.
  • They add a watermark or limit the export, which defeats the point of a free tool.

A proper Hinglish mode transcribes the audio, understands that the speech is Hindi, and writes the result in the Roman spelling people actually use.

Style rules that make Hinglish captions readable

For vertical video (Reels, Shorts, TikTok)

  • 2–3 words per line, maximum two lines on screen.
  • Keep captions inside the middle-safe area — the bottom 15–20% is covered by platform buttons and the caption field.
  • Use a bold sans-serif with a dark stroke or a semi-transparent box; thin fonts disappear over busy footage.
  • Highlight one word at a time instead of colouring the whole sentence.
  • Capitalise the first word only; ALL CAPS reads as shouting and is harder to scan.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing scripts. Devanagari and Roman in the same video looks inconsistent — pick one.
  • Transcribing background music. Lower the music before upload, or fix the stray lines in the editor.
  • Ignoring names. Spellings of people and brands are the errors viewers notice first.
  • Posting without watching the export. Check the final MP4 on a phone once; safe areas look different on a desktop preview.
